# 09 — 2024–2026 Updates (Helpful Content, Core Updates, Discover, AI Overviews)

## SEO Starter Guide refresh (Feb 2024)

Google simplified its beginner guidance — useful signal about what actually matters:
- **Removed** the structured-data section ("advanced topic, beginners don't need it; CMS plugins handle it").
- **Reduced** image guidance to essentially **alt text**.
- **Removed** the dedicated 404 guidance ("do whatever makes sense for users").
- **Removed** the standalone navigation section → emphasized **aggressively internal-linking important URLs**.
- **Removed** the mobile-friendliness section (most platforms are already responsive).
- **Added** a "common SEO myths" section — anti-patterns to ignore.

## Core updates & Helpful Content

- ⚠️ Core updates are **broad ranking-system recalibrations run several times a year**, *not* penalties targeting your site. A drop is often **relative** (others improved), not a strike.
- **Helpful content system** → announced in Aug 2022 as the "Helpful Content Update"; in **March 2024 it became part of the core ranking systems** and no longer exists as a separate system (`docs/14`). "Hit by HCU" now means "hit by a core update".
- **Mar 2024 core update** → Google projected **40% less** low-quality, unoriginal content in results and reported **45%** on completion; shipped with new spam policies (scaled content abuse, expired domain abuse, site reputation abuse — `docs/14`); 45-day rollout, the longest on record.
- **Aug 2024 core update** → rewarded quality, demoted low-value SEO content.
- **Site Reputation Abuse** policy (May 2024, updated Nov 2024) → targets "parasite SEO" (established domains hosting third-party content). Since **30 Aug 2026**, its manual actions no longer apply to searches **in the EEA** (`docs/14`).
- ⚠️ **Not every core update is announced.** Google runs smaller core updates continuously without announcing them, so improvements can register **between** named updates — you don't necessarily have to wait for the next big one.

### Measuring a core update's impact

- ✅ Wait until the rollout is **complete**, then **at least one more full week**, before analyzing in Search Console. Compare that week with **the week before the rollout began** — not with the rollout period itself, when rankings are still moving.
- ⚠️ Practical caveat (not Google guidance): if the post-update week falls on a seasonal peak or trough — Black Friday, holidays, a sale, an exam season — a week-over-week comparison measures the season, not the update. Compare year-over-year for the same weeks, or against a control set of pages the update is unlikely to have touched (`docs/15`).
- ✅ Look at **which pages and query types** moved, not just the site total. A drop concentrated in one template or section points at that section's quality.

### Recovery

Don't make rumor-driven "quick fixes" or delete content as a first move. Focus site-wide on **helpful, reliable, people-first** content; use Google's *creating-helpful-content* self-assessment (`docs/14`). Recovery can take **months** — often visible at the next core update, though it can come earlier through the unannounced smaller ones.

## Discover core update (Feb 2026)

Discover now has **its own core updates**, separate from Search. The first one started **5 Feb 2026**, for English-language users in the US first, with expansion to all countries and languages announced for the following months. Google's stated goals:

- more **locally relevant** content from websites based in the user's country;
- less **sensational content and clickbait**;
- more **in-depth, original and timely** content from websites with expertise in a given area.

What this means in practice:

- ⚠️ **Discover and Search move independently.** A Discover drop with stable Search rankings is not a Search problem — diagnose it with the Discover performance report, not the Search one (`docs/15`).
- ✅ **No special markup is needed.** A page is eligible if it is indexed and meets Discover's content policies; eligibility is not a guarantee.
- ✅ Use **large images**: at least **1200 px wide**, over 300,000 pixels, 16:9, enabled with `max-image-preview:large` (`docs/01`). Keep the important part of the image inside the `og:image` crop.
- ✅ Titles and headlines that **capture the essence** of the content.
- ❌ Clickbait, misleading previews, and teasers that hide crucial information — this is now an explicit target of the Discover system, not just a policy note.
- ✅ Monitor **Search Console → Performance → Discover**; it only shows data once a site passes a minimum impression threshold.

## AI Overviews / AI Mode (formerly SGE)

- ✅ **No separate opt-in.** To appear, a page must simply be **indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet** — same eligibility as normal Search.
- ⚠️ **No special files or markup are needed or used:** Google ignores `llms.txt`, "AI text" files, and Markdown versions. Structured data is **not required** for AI features.
- ✅ Write for humans — well-organized, original, expertise-driven content beats commodity/AI-rehashed text. No need to chop content into tiny "AI chunks."
- Practical signals (third-party measurements, not Google guarantees): AI Overview sources overwhelmingly come from the **top 10 organic results** — so classic ranking remains the prerequisite. List/bulleted formatting is frequently cited. Being cited can **lift CTR**; an AI Overview without your citation can **lower** it.

## E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust)

- ⚠️ **Not a direct ranking factor** or algorithm — it's the framework Google's quality raters use, and it shapes how the helpful-content and core-update systems weigh a page. Don't treat it as a checkbox, but these signals are cheap to add and correlate with what raters (and the algorithms trained to approximate them) reward:
  - ✅ Visible **author byline** on content pages (article/blog/guide), linking to an author bio with real credentials/experience.
  - ✅ A substantive **About** page and easy-to-find **contact/business information** — especially important for YMYL (Your Money or Your Life: health, finance, legal, safety) content.
  - ✅ First-hand **experience signals** where relevant (original photos/data/testing) over rehashed summaries of other sources.
  - ✅ Cite sources for factual claims; keep authorship/ownership unambiguous (no unattributed or misleading bylines).

## The durable takeaway

None of this replaces fundamentals. Ship indexable, server-rendered, well-structured, original content with correct canonical/hreflang/robots/sitemap/structured-data — then AI features and core updates take care of themselves.
